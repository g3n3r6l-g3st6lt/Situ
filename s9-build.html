How S9 is built

Author

Mohamed Gharbia

Published

October 17, 2026

Blueprint

The ledger

Kind Rule Source or reason
E01 evidence How many residents of each building commute, and how S7: census 2021 by tract, corrected once for working from home
E02 evidence When residents leave for work and workers arrive, by mode S7: census 2021 hour bands by tract
E03 evidence The streets a person may walk, and the doors out of the area S1
E04 evidence Which buildings hold business premises, and how many S4: tax roll
E05 evidence How much can be seen from each walkable street S8
A01 assumption Residents who walk to work outside the area head for Place Ville Marie, through the door that makes the whole walk shortest downtown holds most of the jobs within walking distance; no open table says where Griffintown’s walkers work
A02 assumption The share of resident walkers who work inside the area is the people who walk to work here divided by the residents who walk to work (0.19) this assumes everyone who walks to a job here lives here, so it is an upper bound
A03 assumption Inside the area, workers go to the buildings with business premises, in proportion to their number jobs per premises are not open
A04 assumption Riders walk to the metro entrance or bus stop nearest their home along S1 S7 measures this; nothing says which stop people choose
A05 assumption Workers who arrive by metro or bus come from the entrance or stop nearest their workplace as A04
A06 assumption Departures and arrivals spread evenly within each census band; a walk falls in the hour it starts or ends the census gives only bands; most walks here take under 20 minutes
A07 assumption A walker passing through a junction is counted there once the city counts crossings per crosswalk
A08 assumption Only these walks happen: no errands, school runs, dog walks, visitors or walks from parking this slice holds commuters only
A09 assumption In every reading, stops and doors are chosen by the shortest walk so that the readings differ only in the route
A10 assumption Reading “open”: cost = length × (1 + 0.5 × (1 − rank of area seen)); a street S8 did not measure counts as middling a hypothesis drawn from isovist studies; the weight is my choice, tried from 0.25 to 2
A11 assumption Reading “straight”: the fewest degrees of turning, with length breaking ties Hillier and Iida (2005)
A12 assumption Commuters by train, bicycle, car and other modes do not walk in this slice their walks to a station or a car are short or unknown
A13 assumption Beyond a door, a walker goes in a straight line to where it is going, and passes a counted intersection of the ring when that line comes within 40 m of it S1 has no streets outside the area; the line shows the general direction only

The readings

All three readings use the same S1 streets, with the same starts and ends. A route is a chain of streets joined at junctions, and a walker may not turn back along the street just walked. “Shortest” adds up metres. “Open” adds up metres weighted by how enclosed each street is: the street with the smallest area seen in S8 counts at 1.5 times its length, and the most open one at its plain length. “Straight” adds up the degrees turned at each junction, from the direction a walker arrives in to the direction of the next street. Length only breaks ties.

The comparison

S9 reads the counts and space syntax only after the flows are made. At each counted junction, it sets each reading’s commuters against the mean number of pedestrians the city counted there at 7 and 8 h. For every walkable street inside the area, it ranks the morning’s flow against walking choice and integration, and gives a rank correlation. Beyond the doors, it sets the commuters’ straight legs against the ring’s weekday counts, next to the expectations I wrote before reading the ring (outputs/s9/expected.csv). The comparison describes. It does not pick a reading.

Tests

Ten checks

Each check asks one plain question of what S9 wrote. Nine must pass, and all nine do. The tenth only reports.

Question Answer
T1 Do the walkers add up to what S7 says, kind by kind, worked out again from the S7 tables? yes, all six kinds
T2 Does every trip have a route in every reading, joined end to end from start to finish? yes: 16,958 trips × 3 readings
T3 Is the “shortest” route really the shortest walk? yes, against S7’s own code, on every 20th trip
T4 Does each reading win by its own rule? yes: “straight” never turns more than another reading, and “open” never walks more than 1.5 times the shortest
T5 Are the flows on streets and junctions the sum of the routes that use them? yes
T6 Were the flows made without the counts and space syntax? yes: run again with both scrambled, no route moves
T7 Does the comparison with the counts follow from the flows and the S5 table? yes: 7 junctions × 2 hours
T8 Is every rule the code names in the ledger, exactly once? yes: 13 assumptions and 5 pieces of evidence
T9 Does the ring comparison follow from the doors, the targets and S5’s ring, on weekdays only? yes, worked out again: 23 sites
T10 What does this slice give? report
TipTesting the tests

I added half a person to one street’s flow at 8 h, and T5 caught it. T6 first failed because it found the words “counts” and “space syntax” in the description at the top of the code. It now reads the code alone.

Stress tests

Change What moves
X1 the same input twice nothing: identical
X2 reading “open” with weights 0.25, 0.5, 1 and 2 12 to 18 % of walkers change route; the share of the count explained stays at 21 to 27 %
X3 walkers to work outside sent to Place Ville Marie, Square-Victoria or Peel the doors change; Robert-Bourassa at Saint-Jacques still gets no one
X4 share of resident walkers who work inside: 0, 0.19, 0.5, 1 share of the count explained: 21 to 32 %; agreement with walking choice: 0.16 to 0.31
X5 reading “straight” with each metre worth 0, 0.02 and 0.1 degrees walkers going over a quarter further drop from 15 % to 7 %
X6 a ring site counts as passed within 20, 40 or 80 m of a commuter’s straight leg 1 to 5 sites reached, 0.4 to 1.5 % of the ring’s count. Across the canal, none at any distance; towards Old Montréal, a handful at 80 m only

No setting I tried brings the commuters above a third of the pedestrians counted. So the gap is in who walks, and the next slices of S9 have to add people (partial).
