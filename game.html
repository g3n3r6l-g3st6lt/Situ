The game, part by part

How the game is built from the systems, one tested part at a time

I build the game the way I built the systems. Each part has one job, reads the parts before it, writes files any engine can read, and keeps a ledger of its decisions and its own tests. A part that is too large to test as one piece is split into subsystems. One part is built: G1 World (verified). Every other part on this page is planned.

From systems to a game says which job each system does in a game. This page says how those jobs become parts, and in what order.

A historical world, Griffintown in the 1920s to 1940s, comes after these parts, in the same frame so the two eras can be compared street by street (planned).

What runs where

Plans and routes are worked out in Python, next to the systems, from a seed: the same seed gives the same day. The engine moves the bodies, frame by frame, with the player present. So every decision an NPC makes can be printed and tested without opening the engine, and the engine holds no rule the ledger lacks.

Rules for every part

Rule Why
No part is tuned on the data that checks it. The counts at 7 junctions are the only measure of people on the street. Fitting the NPCs to them would leave nothing to test them with.
Space syntax never steers an NPC. A run that uses it is a separate, labelled reading. The same rule as S9: S2 stays a measure.
Missing stays missing. 73 buildings have no height in any source. They stay unknown, in the files and on screen.
Variation comes from differences between people. Random jitter looks lively and explains nothing. Each NPC differs in plan, pace and route preference, and each difference has a source or a ledger row.
Every stop and every change of route has a reason the inspector can print. A gathering with no mechanism behind it is decoration.
