Present Past · 1912 to c1927 Compare in the Lab

Asks Which buildings stood in Griffintown, what they were built of, and how tall they were, read from the 1912 atlas and the 1918 insurance plan.

Found On three blocks between Colborne and Dalhousie, William and Ottawa: 110 outlines on the 1912 atlas and 192 on the May 1918 plan. 25 are carried to the represented period, c1927, after each was read against the 1949 plans.

Stands partial. Readings: assumption. The outlines are traced from the plans’ coloured fills by a script, and their construction is read from the fill colour before the legend has been read. Whether each outline is still drawn in 1949 is read on the 1949 plan, and waits for my review.

Leaves out The rest of the study area, which is not traced yet: there, an empty map means not read, never no building. Storeys and uses for most 1918 outlines.

In the game Not yet.

Trace from the historical workspace by tools/hist_import.py · 1912 outlines · 1918 outlines · carried to c1927 · sources

What H-S4 does

H-S4 is S4 for the past. It reads each building’s outline from the plans, with what the plan says about it: its construction, and on the 1918 insurance plan its storeys, use and occupant. The plans are registered onto today’s ground, so the outlines can be laid over today’s buildings.

So far it covers the three pilot blocks of the historical workspace: Colborne to Shannon, Shannon to Ann, and Ann to Dalhousie, each between William and Ottawa.

On the 1912 atlas, 55 outlines are brick or stone and 55 frame: this atlas does not tell brick from stone. On the 1918 plan, 95 are brick, 95 frame and 2 stone or concrete. The 1918 plan prints storeys and uses; they are read so far for 33 outlines. 9 outlines of 1918 sit inside a larger one: additions, or sheds in yards. Of the 25 carried to c1927, 18 are brick, 6 frame and 1 stone or concrete, as read on the 1918 plan.

How it is read

  • Outlines. A script follows the coloured fill of each building and cuts it along the plan’s ink lines (decision D017). It works at the scan’s resolution, about 5 cm per pixel. Buildings left uncoloured on the plan, such as open sheds and platforms, are not traced.
  • Construction. Read from the fill colour by the usual key of these plans: pink brick, yellow frame, blue stone or concrete (D016, D018). The legend sheets have not been read yet, so the key is provisional.
  • Place. Each outline sits where its plan’s registration puts it: to about 3.5 m on the 1912 plate, 2.5 to 2.6 m on the 1918 sheets. The 1949 sheet 51-70 is registered on ten junctions of William and Ottawa to about 2.0 m, and the two Colborne junctions, kept out of the fit as checks, land 3.9 and 4.5 m from today’s (D040). The east part of the Ann–Dalhousie block is on the next sheet, 52-70. That sheet is drawn stretched along William, so it is fitted together with 51-70, on four junctions and on the points where William and Ottawa cross the edge the two sheets share: to about 3.9 m, and the shared edge meets within 1.7 m (D041).
  • Carried to c1927. A 1918 outline is carried to the represented period only when two things hold (D039). First, the 1927 directory ties its occupant to the same firm, or it was drawn on both the 1912 and 1918 maps and one of its addresses is listed in 1927 (the earlier rule, D020). Second, it is still drawn on the city’s 1949 land-use plans, so 1918 and 1949 bracket the 1920s. 62 of the 183 main 1918 outlines pass the first test. Each of the 62 is laid on the 1949 sheets and read: 25 are still drawn there, 33 partly (a building covers the place, and its walls do not follow the 1918 outline), 2 not, and 2 cannot be told. The 25 are carried. Many of the outlines read partly stand inside one larger 1949 building, such as the Robertson works on Ann, which the 1949 plan draws as one outline where the 1918 plan draws several. Every value carried is inferred, and an outline that is not carried is unknown in c1927, never absent.

How sure

In an audit of the historical workspace, 32 outlines drawn at random all followed a building drawn on the plan. That check was made by the same reader as the tracing, so the outlines stand as an assumption until I have reviewed them (what the words mean). The 1949 readings were made by the same reader and stand the same way. The 1949 plan carries later notes written over some buildings, such as “démolie”, so a reading can take a later state for the 1949 one. Large lettering across a wall leaves notched edges in a few outlines, visible in the middle map.
