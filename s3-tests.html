S3 tests

Author

Mohamed Gharbia

Published

October 10, 2026

Ten checks

Each check asks one plain question of the rules S3 writes. Eight of them must pass, and all eight do. The other two only report. The Lab shows the latest results.

Question Answer
T1 Does every street a car can arrive along have exactly one approach? yes
T2 Does every sign end up with a street, or a written reason it has none? yes
T3 Does every stop sign stand where a driver would see it? yes
T4 Do the turns fit the street layout, and does every forbidden turn have a reason? No street may be a trap. yes
T5 Does each junction’s control agree with its approaches? yes
T6 Do the no-entry signs agree with the city’s one-way streets? report: 67 of 78 agree, and none disagrees; the rest could not be confirmed either way
T7 Does every rule cite the signs behind it? yes
T8 How sure is each link between a sign and a street? report: see S3 Rules
T9 Does every light stand within 25 m of its junction and name at least one of its streets? yes
T10 Does every speed match the city file, and does every gap have a reason? yes

A disagreement in T6 goes on the list of places to check, because I cannot tell from the desk which source is right. The photos explained the last four (S3 checks with photos).

TipTesting the tests

A test that always passes proves nothing. Before trusting T9 and T10, I broke the results on purpose. I moved one light by 30 m and changed two speed cells: one from 30 to 50, and one empty cell to 0. T9 caught the light and T10 caught both speeds. Then I put the files back.

Stress tests

Each stress test changes one thing on purpose and counts how many rules move. The first, X1, simply runs S3 twice on the same input. The results are identical.

X2 moves every sign post at random, by up to 1, 2, 4 and 8 m. Up to 2 m almost nothing changes. At 4 m ten streets change their stop, and at 8 m, 28 do. So the city’s sign positions would have to be off by more than 2 m before the stop rules start to shift. I do not know yet how accurate they are, and that is worth checking on the ground at a few corners.

X3 and X4 change how far before the junction, and how far to the right, a sign may stand. Both sit on a plateau: a little more or less than my 35 m and 20 m changes nothing, so the result does not hang on my exact choice. Only the tightest values lose signs. Most of the signs without a street stand at exits or beyond the edge.

X5 places turn signs by position alone, as my first version did. That leaves 25 streets with no turn at all, against none with the meaning test and the photo decisions. That is why the meaning test exists.

X6 changes how close a light must be to its junction. From 25 to 40 m the same 31 junctions get lights. At 5 m eight are lost: those lights stand 5 to 10 m from their junctions, mostly on Robert-Bourassa and on edge streets, where the city places the light on a wide corner. At 80 m, lights start landing on the wrong junctions.

X7 changes how far a same-name line may lie when a street is missing from the speed file. It is the one setting with no plateau below it. Under 5 m, two Wellington pieces open to cars lose their limit. From 5 m on, all three redrawn pieces are covered. The 5 m was chosen for those three segments, and how S3 is built says so. Autoroute 10 and its ramps are never in the file, at any distance.

Try them in the Lab

X2, X6 and X7 are sliders in the Lab. Move the signs or change a distance, and see which streets change.
