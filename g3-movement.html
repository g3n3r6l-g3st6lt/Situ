G3 Movement

How NPCs get from one activity to the next, on foot and by car

Asks Which way does each NPC go, and how does it move through the street with others around it?

Reads G1 ground and sidewalks; G2 plans; S3 rules; S9 route readings; the city’s parking signage (to download).

Writes A route per trip (in Python), and in the engine a log of every wait, stop and change of route with its reason.

Stands planned.

Leaves out Space syntax, by rule. Whether drivers and walkers keep the rules, until observed.

Trace The game, part by part

Why movement is split

Movement is the largest part, so it is split in five. One subsystem chooses routes, and runs in Python before the game starts. The other four move bodies frame by frame, in the engine, because they depend on who else is in the street, the player included.

Subsystem Job Runs in Main source Status of the rule
G3.1 Route choice the route of each trip Python S9’s three readings: shortest, open, straight each NPC weights the readings its own way; the spread of weights is an assumption
G3.2 Walking walk inside the sidewalks, avoid others, cross at crossings engine G1 surfaces and crossing marks walking speeds from published studies (to choose)
G3.3 Junctions wait for lights and stop signs engine S3 control; light timing estimated from the counts timing an assumption where the city did not count
G3.4 Driving follow the car ahead, keep to lanes, obey turns and limits engine S3 banned moves and speed limits; the Intelligent Driver Model the model is published (Treiber, Hennecke and Helbing 2000); its settings are assumptions
G3.5 Parking look for a legal place, and circle when none is free engine the city’s parking signage an assumption until observed

Movement that varies for a reason

Crowds in games often look rigid for reasons that can be named. Each has a fix with a source behind it.

Why it looks rigid The fix
everyone leaves on the hour departures spread across S7’s leaving-hour ranges (an assumption on the spread inside each range)
everyone walks at one speed a speed for each NPC, from published walking-speed studies
everyone takes the same shortest route each NPC weights S9’s readings in its own way
everyone walks on the street’s centreline walking inside G1’s sidewalk surfaces
NPCs either walk or stand still states with reasons: waiting at a light, queueing at a stop, sitting (G4)
everyone keeps every rule crossing against the light at a rate, marked as an assumption until observed

Random jitter is not on the list. It makes a crowd look lively and explains nothing.

Why space syntax stays out

S2 measures how each street sits in the layout. If it steered the NPCs, G5 could no longer use it to test them: agreement would be built in. A run where NPCs read S2 is allowed as a separate, labelled reading, compared with the others.

Without City Sample

Epic’s City Sample has tuned traffic, but its cars and drivers depend on the sample’s own code. An earlier prototype of mine tried to move them into another project, and the transfer failed. Its junction logic also made both streets take turns at the 42 junctions where only the side street has a stop sign. G3.4 therefore builds its own driving on the engine’s general tools, and follows S3 as the city files it. See G7 Engine.

Tests (planned)

  • Every trip’s route is one of the readings it was given, and can be printed with its weights.
  • No car makes a move S3 bans; no car exceeds the street’s limit.
  • Every stop in a run has a reason in the log.
  • A run’s counts at the 7 junctions are compared with S5 by G5, and never used to tune G3.
