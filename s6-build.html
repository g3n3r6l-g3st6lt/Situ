How S6 is built and tested

Author

Mohamed Gharbia

Published

October 16, 2026

Blueprint

The sources

Source What S6 takes from it
Ville de Montréal, Arbres publics sur le territoire de la Ville each public tree: species, trunk diameter at chest height, planting date, street or park
Ville de Montréal, Bornes d’incendie each fire hydrant, with its position, status and installation date
Ville de Montréal, Trottoir et îlot (road asset database) each sidewalk piece and traffic island: outline, material, year built, curb extension, cycling use
Ville de Montréal, Entraves et travaux en cours the road works listed on the day I saved the file
OpenStreetMap benches, waste baskets, bicycle racks, picnic tables, fountains, shelters, drinking water, street lamps, surveillance cameras, crossings
Mapillary map features (graph.mapillary.com), fetched 2026-10-16 each street light, surveillance camera and painted crossing its software found in photos, with the dates of the first and last photo; its hydrants, benches, bins and racks only to measure how often it agrees with the other sources

The city’s files are open under CC BY 4.0 and OpenStreetMap under ODbL. Mapillary’s map features are under CC BY-SA 4.0. The large city files are clipped once to the study area, and S6 reads only the clipped copy. A point is kept when it lies inside the study area, and a sidewalk or island when its centre does, the rule S4 uses for buildings.

Values as the source gives them

S6 copies each value and changes none. A tree with no planting date keeps an empty date and the reason, and a bench with no tags in OpenStreetMap keeps none. A hydrant whose installation date is the city’s placeholder, 1 January 1900, keeps no date.

Objects found in photos

Mapillary’s software may place one object twice, or see a pole’s two heads as two lights. So S6 joins detections of one kind lying within 3 m of each other, in a chain, and places the object at their mean. Each object keeps every detection it holds, with the dates of the first and last photo that show it. Zebra stripes and plain crosswalk lines both count as a crossing.

An object from the photos and one of the same kind in OpenStreetMap less than 5 m apart count as one object, and each names the other. An object the software has found in no photo since 2023 becomes a checkpoint, a case set aside for a closer look.

The street each object stands on

Each object belongs to the nearest S1 segment, a stretch of street, within 30 m. An object farther away has no street and says why: most are trees in parks, or benches and tables along the canal path. The stress test below shows that the distance only decides whether an object has a street, never which one.

Width of a sidewalk

The city draws sidewalks as outlines and gives no width. S6 estimates a mean width as twice the area divided by the perimeter. This is exact for a long thin strip and a little low for short pieces. The typical (median) piece is about 2.3 m wide. A piece wider than 10 m on average would be listed for a look, since it is more likely a plaza or a joined drawing; none is.

Assumptions

Assumption Why I accept it for now What would replace it
an object belongs to the nearest street within 30 m sidewalks, trees and hydrants stand beside their street a link drawn by hand where it matters
twice the area over the perimeter is a sidewalk’s width the city gives outlines only widths measured on site or from the curb lines
the road works listed on one day stand for that day only the city’s list changes daily a copy of the list saved each week
detections of one kind less than 3 m apart are one object a pole often carries two heads, and the software may place one object twice objects counted on a walk along a few streets
a detection within 5 m of a recorded object is that object Mapillary’s hydrants stand a median 0.9 m from the city’s a closer look at the photos where the two disagree
an object found in no photo since 2023 may be gone the area has been rebuilt since newer photos, or a walk

Tests

Nine checks

Each check asks one plain question of what S6 wrote. Seven must pass, and all seven do; the last two only report.

Question Answer
T1 Is every tree, hydrant, piece of furniture, OpenStreetMap point, road work, sidewalk and island here exactly once? yes, 2,898 objects and 519 surfaces
T2 Does every object, and the centre of every surface, lie inside the study area? yes
T3 Are the trees’ species, trunk sizes, planting dates and positions copied as the city gives them? yes; 196 trees without a planting date stay empty
T4 Is every object linked to its nearest S1 street within 30 m, or does it say why not? yes; 156 stand farther, each with its reason
T5 Can every surface’s area and estimated width be rebuilt from its outline? yes
T6 Is every road work past its permit, and every object found in no photo since 2023, a checkpoint, and nothing else? yes, 5 works and 35 objects from photos
T7 Is every detection in exactly one object of its kind, and is every match with OpenStreetMap mutual? yes; 1,035 detections make 900 objects
T8 What do the sources cover? report
T9 How often do Mapillary’s detections stand within 5 m of an object a person recorded? report: hydrants 68 of 82, crossings 74 of 102, cameras 2 of 34
TipTesting the tests

T5 failed on its first run. S6 measured each sidewalk from its full-precision outline, then stored the outline rounded to the centimetre, so the stored outline gave a slightly different area. S6 now measures the outline it stores.

Stress tests

Run twice on the same input, S6 gives the same output (X1). X2 changes the link distance. At 10 m, 644 objects have no street; at 20 m, 294; at 30 m, 156; at 50 m, 75. No object ever moves to another street, so the distance decides only how far an object may stand from its street. Of the 75 objects still without one at 50 m, 39 are benches, tables, bins and racks, and 28 are street lights, most likely in parks or along the canal. The other 8 are park trees.

X3 changes the distance within which detections of one kind become one object, from none to 8 m. Lights go from 877 to 628 and crossings from 111 to 73, while cameras barely change. The number of lights depends on this rule more than on anything else in S6. The matches with OpenStreetMap hold up to 5 m, then fall a little.
