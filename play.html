From systems to play

How the systems inform the game’s design, and what they cannot give it

The systems describe Griffintown as its open files and street photos record it. The game is where that description would meet people: players, and the residents whose accounts the systems lack. Nothing on this page is built or found. Each row is a question I could put to play, and every one is planned.

I treat the move from system to game as a translation. A system is a source. I read it, decide what can become a rule or a space, and build that into the game’s grammar. Then I read the play back, and the play becomes a source in turn. Part of a system is lost in the move, and play adds things no system held (assumption: this is how I choose to work, and the questions below test it).

Four kinds of knowledge

Kind In Situ now Status What it needs
present-day data: city files, OpenStreetMap, street photos, the census S1 to S8 verified or partial, system by system already traced (Tracing a result)
historical evidence: Griffintown in the 1920s to 1940s not yet in any system planned historical maps and records
model inference: what S9 imagines S9’s morning on foot assumption comparison with counts, then fieldwork
what residents know: routines and memories nothing yet planned fieldwork: interviews and focus groups, after research ethics approval

None of these stands in for another. A simulated walk is not a resident’s routine, and a space syntax value is not a feeling about a street.

System by system

System Represents Leaves out A question for play What would answer it
S1 Streets where one may walk or drive, as the city files it shortcuts nobody filed; streets closed for a while When the network becomes the game’s ground, do players find the paths the city file missed? play logs of routes; my observation on site
S2 Scores how each street sits in the whole layout what people do there Without being told, do players drift towards the streets with high choice, as the idea of natural movement proposes (space syntax)? play routes compared with S2, the same test S9 runs
S3 Rules stops, lights, turns and speeds whether the rules are kept If players must wait at lights and stop at signs, does a junction’s beat appear in play? play timing at junctions, set against the city’s counts
S4 Buildings what each building is used for, and how many live in it what a place means, and what happens upstairs Which buildings become destinations in play, and are they the ones residents name? play logs, and interviews
S5 Rhythm busy hours that were counted, open hours that were promised weekends on foot, and lingering Can a game day carry the counted shape of a weekday, and can players read the hour from the street? counts on weekends; play sessions at different game hours
S6 Streetscape the objects on the street private trees; how the objects are used Do benches, lights and cameras work as landmarks or as reasons to stop, for players and for residents? interviews, and play logs
S7 Commuters who commutes, how and when, from the 2021 census every trip that is not a commute, and every route What changes when the player is given a census commuter’s trip, compared with free walking? two versions of one play session
S8 Views what can be seen from each point of the streets attention, and everything that blocks a view besides buildings Do open and closed views change where players pause or turn? play logs of pauses, and observation of where people linger
S9 Observer an imagined morning, built on stated assumptions most of the people in the street When S9’s walkers fill a game street, can players tell the model’s gaps from Griffintown’s, such as the empty Robert-Bourassa junction? play sessions with the ledger at hand, and interviews

Which claims need which evidence

A claim about Needs In Situ
how a street is laid out or regulated the city’s files and photos done, S1 to S3
how many people pass, and when counts and observation partly: nine counted weekdays, three bicycle detectors
what a place means to the people who use it interviews and focus groups not yet
how Griffintown was historical maps and records not yet
whether play carries something of Griffintown playtesting, read against all of the above not yet

What waits

Nothing that involves residents or players gets built before the questions and the ethics approval are in place. Until then, the systems stay descriptions of the files, and the game stays planned.
