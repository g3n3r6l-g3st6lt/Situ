From systems to a game

What each system supplies, the job it does in the game, and what it cannot supply

The systems describe Griffintown. A game needs them for four jobs: a world to stand in, people moving through it on time, places where something can happen, and a way to check the result. Each system does one of these jobs first. This page says which, with the counts each system holds today.

I treat the move from a system to the game as a translation. A system is the source. Each decision that turns part of it into a game element is written down, with its kind (source observation, researcher decision, assumption or gap) and an alternative. The first part of that translation is built: G1 World (verified). The rest is planned.

Four jobs

Job What the game needs Systems What no system supplies
World ground, buildings and objects to stand among S1, S4, S6 roofs, facades, interiors, terrain, entrances
Rhythm people and vehicles that move by rules, at the right hours S3, S5, S7, S9 every trip besides a commute; evenings; weekends on foot
Points places where people could stop or gather, or the player could act S4, S5, S6, S7, S8 any record of people gathering
Check measures the game’s movement is compared with S2, S5, S9 counts beyond seven junctions and 23 sites

S5 appears twice on purpose. Its opening hours time the people in the game. Its counts stay out of the game, so they can check it.

These systems become the ground and the masses the player moves among. The NPCs, the people and vehicles the game moves by its own rules, use the same ground.

Rhythm

These systems would run the NPCs: who is out, when, by which mode, under which rules, and by which route. A city’s rhythm in the game can only be as complete as these sources. Today they cover commuting on a weekday morning.

The prototype

The translation is built as code next to the systems, in the same way: each step reads the systems’ outputs, writes files any engine can read, and keeps a ledger and tests. No engine is chosen at this stage.

Step Job Status Reads

G2 Rhythm NPC schedules and routes planned S3, S5, S7, S9
G3 Points candidate stops and interactions planned S4, S5, S6, S7, S8

What no system gives the game

Two kinds of knowledge are missing from every system. Griffintown in the 1920s to 1940s needs historical maps and records. What residents know needs interviews and focus groups, after research ethics approval. Until then, the game can hold Griffintown’s present layout and its weekday commute, and every other part of its rhythm is an assumption the ledger names.
