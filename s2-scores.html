S2 Scores

Author

Mohamed Gharbia

Published

October 10, 2026

What S2 does

S2 takes the space syntax values and attaches them to every S1 segment, and it records which analysed line each value came from. Later systems never read these values. Only S8 Observer uses them, at the very end, as a measuring stick (why).

Two kinds of value matter most here. Choice is roughly how often a street lies on the way between other places. Integration is roughly how easy a street is to reach. Both are measured within a radius, from 200 m up to the whole island.

How I built it is on How S2 is built, and the checks are on S2 tests.

How a value is found

The values come from PlanX’s analysis of the walking network and the driving network, where each analysed line carries its own values. For every S1 segment I look for the analysed lines lying under it, within 1 m. If two pieces of the same street lie under one segment, its value is their mean, weighted by length. A segment that finds too little gets no value at all, never a zero, and a written reason.

Result

257 of 264 segments with walking values · 227 with vehicle values

Almost every segment found its street exactly: 251 match one analysed line. Six are split across two pieces of the same street, such as Rue des Seigneurs and Rue Wellington, because the walking network cut those lines where a path joins and S1 did not.

Seven segments have no walking value. All seven are Autoroute 10 and its ramps. Highways were left out of the walking network, so their values stay empty.

Thirty-seven segments have no vehicle value, and none should. They are walking paths and connectors, or city streets cars may not use, like the Allées or the pedestrian ends of Murray and Smith. No segment closed to cars carries a vehicle value. Before October there were 31, because the older Sample 4 vehicle analysis included pedestrian ways.

What changed in October

On 10 October I reran both analyses on the rebuilt networks and compared each segment inside Griffintown with its old value. Most streets kept their place in the order: the rank agreement (Spearman’s correlation, where 1.00 means no street moved) was 0.99 or 1.00 for every measure I compared.

Two changes are real, and both come from my own decisions. Rue Brennan and Rue Ann gained walking choice: the east end of Brennan went from 0.00 to 0.90, and Ann from 0.37 to 0.93. Both now lead somewhere, because I assumed they connect to the paths beyond them. That is an assumption. If a photo shows those streets really stop, I will reject those decisions and the values will fall back.

Murray and Smith lost vehicle choice. The driving network leaves out their pedestrian ends, by the same road rule S1 uses. In the analysis cars can no longer pass through there, so the street before carries less through-traffic.

Watch the scale change

In the Lab, open the S2 tab and move the distance slider, or press play. You see walking or driving choice or integration as the radius grows from 200 m to the whole island. Each street is coloured by its rank in Griffintown, from grey for the lowest to pink for the highest. Click a street to read all its values.

Maps

S2 holds 13 values for every street. The maps show four: walking at 800 m, about a ten-minute walk, and driving at 2000 m, a short trip by car. I chose those radii myself. 400 to 800 m is the usual low radius in space syntax studies (van Nes & Yamu 2021, p. 65), and Hillier, Yang and Turner (2012, p. 165) found 2000 m often significant in city work. Neither source ties 2000 m to cars, and neither radius comes from Griffintown’s data. Every other value is in the Lab.

What the values say

These are readings of the layout. They say nothing yet about where people actually walk.

Measure Highest in Griffintown Lowest
walking choice, 800 m des Seigneurs, Guy, de la Commune, de la Montagne, Notre-Dame Saint-Thomas, Eleanor, Saint-Martin, Olier, Dalhousie
walking integration, 800 m Notre-Dame, Robert-Bourassa, Guy, Georges-Vanier, William canal path, Olier, Square-Gallery, Smith, Brennan
walking choice, whole island Notre-Dame, Guy, de la Montagne, Peel, des Seigneurs Saint-Thomas, Olier, Eleanor, Rioux, Dalhousie
vehicle choice, 2000 m Notre-Dame, Robert-Bourassa, Guy, des Seigneurs, de la Montagne Olier, Square-Gallery, Saint-Thomas, Brennan, Eleanor

Each street is averaged over its segments inside Griffintown, weighted by length. Streets shorter than 100 m are left out, and so are connectors and footways.

My first reading, and it is an interpretation: the long north-south streets (Guy, de la Montagne, des Seigneurs, Peel) carry the most through-movement potential, on foot and by car. The canal path is easy to pass along and hard to reach, with low integration at 800 m. S8 will test whether simulated and real movement agree.

Notes

Values from different networks are different measurements. The older Sample 4 values are kept for reference and never mixed with these.
