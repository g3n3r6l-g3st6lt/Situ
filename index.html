Situ

Situ is the prototype of my PhD research-creation project. It asks whether a game-like 3D experience can pass on and archive the spatial culture and urban rhythm of Griffintown, Montréal.

I build it as small systems from the city's open data, each tested before the next one uses it. Eight systems are verified: streets, space syntax scores, traffic rules, buildings, hours of the week, street objects, commuters and views. The ninth is partial: it imagines a weekday morning on foot and compares it with the city's counts.

Nothing is archived yet, and the game itself is planned. Everything so far comes from open files and street images, read at my desk. The counts cover nine weekdays at seven junctions, and the people who live here have not yet been asked. Limits

Research

Space syntax measures how a street network is laid out. It is one of my measuring sticks, next to the city's own counts. The simulated people never read it. I compare where they go with what it predicts, street by street.

Integration measures how easy a street is to reach. Choice measures how often it lies on the way between other places.

Data

One walking network for the whole island: the city's streets joined with the off-street paths people use. Every source has its date and licence.

47,197 lines

I took out highways and streets that exist only on plans. I added 161 km of the city's own off-street paths.

A line is one piece of a street or a path in the network file, as analysed on 10 October 2026. The number says how the island is drawn. It says nothing about how much anyone walks.
