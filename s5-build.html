How S5 is built and tested

Author

Mohamed Gharbia

Published

October 15, 2026

Blueprint

From quarter-hours to hours

The city counts in 15-minute slices, by kind of road user. S5 keeps every slice counted within 15 m of an S1 junction, the same rule as the counts page. It adds them up by mode, keeping people on foot and bicycles apart from motor vehicles of every kind. A slice with nobody in it stays a counted zero, which differs from a slice nobody counted.

An hour gets a value only when all four of its quarters were counted. Several one-day windows open or close on the half hour, so their edge hours are partial. Sixteen hours at the junctions, 48 rows across the modes, stay empty, each noting how many quarters it had. S5 never stretches part of an hour into a whole one.

The day’s shape

A busy junction and a quiet one can share a rhythm, so S5 compares each day with itself. The reference hours are those counted in full on every day: 7h, 8h, 11h, 12h, 16h and 17h. For each mode, each day is divided by its own mean over those six hours, so 1.0 is an ordinary daytime hour of that day. Two of the six fall in each of the city’s windows, so no window outweighs another.

A weekday hour takes the mean of every day that counted it, and keeps their lowest and highest values. Each value names the days behind it, so it can be rebuilt by hand. Outside the reference hours, most hours rest on J040 alone, and S5 marks them.

Only Tuesdays to Thursdays were counted. S5 lets their shape stand in for Monday and Friday, and says so on every such row. Saturday and Sunday stay empty, with the reason.

Bicycle detectors

The city’s permanent bicycle detectors report every hour, for each site by direction and lane. S5 keeps the detectors that stand inside the study area, by the same test S4 uses for buildings: Peel at Ottawa, northbound and southbound, and William at Nazareth, westbound. Two detectors on Peel at Notre-Dame stand 35 and 49 m from junction J187, outside the area. S5 lists them without using them.

A recorded hour of 0 stays 0, and a missing hour is a gap. A detector’s day counts only when all 24 hours were recorded. The Griffintown day adds up the three detectors on the days when every one is complete: 376 days. Each such day is divided by its own mean over the same reference hours as the counts, then grouped by season and by kind of day (weekday, Saturday, Sunday). A group keeps its mean and its middle half, the range of the central half of its days.

The seasons follow whole months, as weather services count them: winter is December to February, spring is March to May, and so on. Holidays are not set apart, so a public holiday on a Monday counts as a weekday.

The ring

A counted intersection joins the ring when it lies outside the study area and within 600 m of its edge. It must also stand more than 15 m from any S1 junction, the distance within which a count belongs to S1. S5 reads its quarter-hours and hours by the same rules as inside, and keeps each site’s mean per hour with weekdays and weekends apart. The ring feeds no shape or table S5 builds, and a test checks this.

Reading opening hours

OpenStreetMap writes hours in a small grammar, like Mo-Fr 07:00-22:00; Sa 08:00-20:00; Su off. S5 reads the part of it Griffintown’s places use. A rule after a semicolon replaces what earlier rules said about the same days; a rule after a comma adds to them. A day the text never names is closed, which is the tag’s own convention. Hours past midnight carry into the next morning, so a bar open 17:00-03:00 on Saturday is open early on Sunday.

An opening with no closing time leaves those hours unknown. So does a note such as “sur rdv” (by appointment), or a text S5 cannot parse. Public holidays are set aside, since S5 has no calendar yet.

The city’s list of public places gives its hours in French, day by day, and sometimes for one season: the play fountains at Place des Aiguilleurs open from 21 May and close from 22 September. S5 keeps the season with the place.

A place counts as open in an hour when it is open at half past. A place with no hours is unknown at every hour, and S5 never marks it closed.

Tests

Fourteen checks

Each check asks one plain question of what S5 wrote. Twelve must pass, and all twelve do; T11 and T12 only report. The Lab shows the latest results.

Question Answer
T1 Does the evidence hold every counted quarter-hour, with the same totals as the city’s file? yes, 1,440 quarters
T2 Does an hour get a value only when all four quarters were counted? yes; 48 partial hours stay empty
T3 Were the reference hours counted every day, and does each day average 1 over them? yes

T5 Do Saturday and Sunday stay empty, with the reason? yes
T6 Is every S4 place here once, and is a place without readable hours unknown at every hour? yes, 610 rows: 558 places and 52 repeats, each pointing to its twin
T7 Does the hours reader give the expected week on fixed examples? yes, 16 examples
T8 Does every place’s week come from its own hours text, and do the counts per hour add up? yes

T12 Do the counts and the detectors agree on a weekday’s bicycles? report: correlation 0.87 in winter, 0.89 over all seasons

TipTesting the tests

A test that always passes proves nothing. So I broke the finished files five ways: I gave Saturday at 8h a value, turned one unknown hour of a place without hours into closed, added one person to one counted hour, filled a partial hour, and changed one letter of a place’s week in the Lab’s copy. Each was caught: the first by T4 and T5, the second by T6 and T8, the third by T1 and T4, the fourth by T1 and T2, and the last by the Lab’s check L7. Then I put the files back.

Stress tests

Run twice on the same input, S5 gives the same output (X1). The other stress tests ask how much the shape depends on so few days.

X2 leaves out one counted day at a time. In the reference hours, which all nine days share, no value moves by more than 0.12, and for people on foot and cars by no more than 0.06. In the hours J040 holds alone, its three December days disagree more. Leaving one out moves people on foot by up to 0.12 and bicycles by up to 0.44, since a few cyclists in an hour make a large share. The bicycle values at night and in mid-afternoon are the weakest numbers in S5.

X3 leaves out one junction at a time. The busiest hour stays 17h for every mode, with one exception: without J040, the bicycles’ busiest hour moves to 8h. So the evening cycling peak leans on J040.

X5 leaves out one month of detector days at a time. Weekday shapes hold well, moving by at most 0.05 in summer and 0.04 in autumn. Weekend shapes rest on fewer days and move more, up to 0.46 for a winter Sunday. Spring is the thinnest season, with 22 weekdays, 8 Saturdays and 9 Sundays.

X4 changes what “open in this hour” means. Of the other readings I tried, the strictest, open for the whole hour, removes 75 of the week’s 5,626 open place-hours, and the others move fewer. The choice of half past hardly matters.
